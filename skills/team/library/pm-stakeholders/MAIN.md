# Stakeholders, communication and the team

## Stakeholders

A **stakeholder** is anyone who affects, is affected by, or thinks they are affected by the
project: sponsor, client, users, team, line managers, suppliers, regulators, unions, the
community. Identify them early and keep the list alive: new stakeholders appear in every phase.

**Context analysis** first: the organization's strategy, culture, constraints, the market and
regulation; a SWOT helps place the project in it.

**Register**: name or group, role, interest in the project, influence, expectations and
concerns, current engagement, desired engagement, strategy, owner of the relationship.

**Power/interest grid**

```text
             low interest        high interest
high power   KEEP SATISFIED      MANAGE CLOSELY
low power    MONITOR             KEEP INFORMED
```

**Engagement levels**: unaware, resistant, neutral, supportive, leading. Plan the actions that
move each key stakeholder from current to desired, and check at every review.

## Communication plan

```markdown
| Audience | Information | Purpose | Format / channel | Frequency | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Steering committee | status, decisions needed | decide | 1-page report + meeting | monthly | PM |
| Team | priorities, blockers | coordinate | stand-up | daily | team lead |
| Users | what changes for them, when | prepare | email + demo | at each release | PO |
```

- **Channels**: with n people, the possible two-way channels are n(n - 1) / 2 (10 people: 45).
  Adding people adds channels much faster, which is why the plan defines who talks to whom.
- **Push** (sent: email, reports), **pull** (fetched: wiki, dashboards), **interactive**
  (meetings, calls): use interactive for decisions and conflict, push and pull for information.
- Write for the audience: decision makers want the conclusion first, the team wants detail.

## Effective communication

- Sender encodes, a medium carries, the receiver decodes; **noise** (jargon, language,
  distraction, distrust) distorts it. Ask for **feedback** to confirm understanding.
- Most of the meaning in a face-to-face conversation comes from tone and body language; written
  messages lose it, so be more explicit in writing.
- **Constructive feedback**: specific behavior, its effect, what you would like instead; in
  private for criticism, timely, about facts.
- **Meetings**: an objective, an agenda with times, the right people only, a chair and a note
  taker; end with decisions, actions, owners and dates; send the minutes within a day.

## Leadership and motivation

- Leadership sets direction and gets commitment; management plans and controls. A project
  manager needs both, and adapts the style to the person and the situation (directing,
  coaching, supporting, delegating).
- **Maslow**: needs in levels (physiological, safety, belonging, esteem, self-actualization);
  a satisfied need no longer motivates.
- **Herzberg**: hygiene factors (pay, conditions, policies) prevent dissatisfaction but do not
  motivate; motivators (achievement, recognition, responsibility, growth) do.
- **McGregor**: Theory X assumes people avoid work and need control; Theory Y assumes they seek
  responsibility when the goal is meaningful. Expectations tend to fulfil themselves.

## Team development

**Tuckman**: forming (polite, unclear roles), storming (conflict over roles and approach),
norming (agreed ways of working), performing (autonomous and effective), adjourning (the team
ends; recognize the work and capture lessons). Help the team through storming instead of
avoiding it: clear roles, a working agreement, early wins.

## Negotiation

Separate the people from the problem; focus on **interests**, not positions; invent options
for mutual gain; use objective criteria. Know your **BATNA** (best alternative to a negotiated
agreement) and estimate theirs: it sets when to walk away. Prepare the zone of possible
agreement and your concessions in advance.

## Conflict

Conflict in projects is normal (priorities, resources, schedules, technical choices,
personalities). Modes (Thomas-Kilmann):

| Mode | Use when |
|---|---|
| Collaborate (problem solving) | the issue matters to both and time allows: the default for lasting solutions |
| Compromise | both sides must give something; a temporary fix |
| Accommodate | the issue matters more to the other side; preserving the relationship |
| Compete (force) | urgent, safety or ethics; others cannot decide |
| Avoid (withdraw) | trivial issues, or to let tempers cool |

## Problem solving

1. Define the problem (facts, not symptoms or blame).
2. Find the root cause (5 whys, cause-and-effect diagram).
3. Generate options.
4. Choose with explicit criteria.
5. Implement with an owner and a date.
6. Check that it worked; record the lesson.

## Ethics and emotional intelligence

Responsibility, respect, fairness and honesty: report the real status, declare conflicts of
interest, respect confidentiality and the law. **Emotional intelligence**: self-awareness,
self-management, social awareness and relationship management; it is what makes the other
skills work under pressure.
