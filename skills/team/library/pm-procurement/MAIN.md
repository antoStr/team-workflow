# Procurement and contracts

## Make or buy

Buy when the skills, capacity or speed are not in house, when a supplier can do it cheaper or
with less risk, or when the work is not core. Make when control, know-how or confidentiality
matter more. Compare the full cost: internal time, management of the supplier, risks,
switching costs later.

## Contract lifecycle

1. **Plan**: what to buy, when, contract type, evaluation criteria, budget.
2. **Statement of work (SOW)**: the work, deliverables, acceptance criteria, schedule,
   standards, reporting; precise enough for a supplier to price it.
3. **Tender and selection**: request for information (RFI), proposal (RFP) or quotation (RFQ);
   evaluate on weighted criteria (technical fit, price or total cost, experience, financial
   stability, delivery capacity, risk); negotiate.
4. **Award**: sign the contract; it is the legal baseline of the relationship.
5. **Administration**: monitor performance, approve deliverables and invoices, run contract
   change control, manage claims and disputes, keep records.
6. **Closure**: verify all deliverables, settle claims and payments, release guarantees,
   evaluate the supplier, archive.

Public procurement follows its own law (for example the public contracts code of each
country): thresholds, publicity, award procedures and deadlines are mandatory, not choices.

## Contract types

| Type | Price | Risk on | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firm fixed price (FFP) | fixed for the defined scope | seller | well-defined scope |
| Fixed price incentive fee (FPIF) | target cost and profit, shared over/underrun up to a ceiling | shared, mostly seller | defined scope, some uncertainty |
| Fixed price with economic price adjustment | fixed, indexed (inflation, materials) | shared | long contracts |
| Cost plus fixed fee (CPFF) | costs + a fixed fee | buyer | uncertain scope, research |
| Cost plus incentive fee (CPIF) | costs + a fee that varies with performance against targets | buyer, shared | uncertain scope, wish to reward efficiency |
| Cost plus award fee (CPAF) | costs + a fee awarded on subjective criteria | buyer | performance hard to measure |
| Time and materials (T&M) | rate x time + materials | buyer | staff augmentation, small or unclear work; add a cap |

The less defined the scope, the more the risk moves to the buyer. Fixed price on an unclear
scope does not remove the risk: it comes back as change requests and claims.

## Contract changes and claims

Every change goes through the contract's change procedure, in writing, with its price and
schedule impact agreed before the work. A **claim** is a disputed request for compensation;
handle it with the records (correspondence, minutes, approvals), then negotiation, mediation or
arbitration as the contract says.

## The project manager's role

The PM defines the needs, the SOW and the acceptance criteria, takes part in selection,
manages the supplier day to day and integrates its work into the project. Legal commitment and
contract signature usually belong to procurement or legal: the PM must not change a contract by
an informal instruction.
